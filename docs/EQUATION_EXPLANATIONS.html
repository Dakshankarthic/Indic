Document Layout Analysis & Transcription

Complete Mathematical Formulations, Variable Definitions & Algorithmic Explanations
EQ 1 Physical Image Degradation & Non-Uniform Illumination
$$I(x,y) = R(x,y) \cdot L(x,y) + \eta(x,y)$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(I(x,y)\): The actual observed pixel intensity in the scanned manuscript image.
  • \(R(x,y)\): The true physical reflectance of the ink and paper substrate (what we want to isolate).
  • \(L(x,y)\): Spatially non-uniform illumination field (shadows, gradient lighting from curvature).
  • \(\eta(x,y)\): Additive noise representing physical decay (mold, ink bleed-through, paper rot).
Why this is needed: Standard OCR fails because it assumes even lighting and clean white paper. This model accounts for real-world lighting gradients and substrate decay mathematically.
EQ 2 Brahmic Connected Orthography (Headline Union)
$$\mathcal{S}_{\text{word}} = \bigcup_{i=1}^{M} \mathcal{G}_i \cup \mathcal{H}_{\text{shirorekha}}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(\mathcal{S}_{\text{word}}\): The complete connected pixel component of a Devanagari word.
  • \(\mathcal{G}_i\): The \(i\)-th individual character glyph (Akshara vertical stem or attached vowel sign).
  • \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{shirorekha}}\): The continuous horizontal top headline binding all characters together.
Why this is needed: In Latin scripts (like English), letters are separated by blank space. In Sanskrit/Devanagari, the Shirorekha binds letters into one long stroke. This formula is the foundation for cutting the headline to isolate letters.
EQ 3 Hierarchical Layout Decomposition Target (PAGE-XML)
$$\mathcal{T} = \left\{ \mathcal{R}_{\text{frame}}, \{\mathcal{R}_{\text{text}}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K}, \{\mathcal{R}_{\text{illus}}^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^{M}, \{\mathcal{R}_{\text{damage}}^{(d)}\}_{d=1}^{D}, \{\mathcal{L}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}, \{\mathcal{W}_{j,p}\}, \{\mathcal{G}_{j,p,q}\} \right\}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(\mathcal{T}\): The full document tree conforming to the PRImA PAGE-XML 2013 standard.
  • \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{frame}}\): Folio boundary polygon; \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{text}}\): Text blocks; \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{illus}}\): Graphics; \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{damage}}\): Punch holes.
  • \(\mathcal{L}_j\): \(j\)-th text line; \(\mathcal{W}_{j,p}\): Word \(p\) in line \(j\); \(\mathcal{G}_{j,p,q}\): Character glyph \(q\).
Why this is needed: Defines layout parsing as a nested tree hierarchy: Page → TextRegion → TextLine → Word → Glyph.
EQ 4 Adaptive Gaussian Thresholding & Morphological Filter
$$B(x,y) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } I_{\text{gray}}(x,y) < \mu_G(x,y) - C \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}, \quad \mu_G(x,y) = (I_{\text{gray}} * G_\sigma)(x,y)$$ $$B_{\text{clean}} = (B \ominus \mathcal{E}_{3\times 3}) \oplus \mathcal{E}_{3\times 3}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(B(x,y)\): Binary ink mask (1 = ink stroke, 0 = background).
  • \(\mu_G(x,y)\): Local weighted mean computed via 2D Gaussian kernel over a \(51 \times 51\) pixel window.
  • \(C = 5\): Threshold margin preventing background noise from turning into black pixels.
  • \(\mathcal{E}_{3\times 3}\): \(3\times 3\) elliptical structuring element used for morphological opening (erosion \(\ominus\) then dilation \(\oplus\)).
Why this is needed: Global thresholding (Otsu) turns dark palm leaves or stained corners completely black. Adaptive Gaussian binarization dynamically calculates the local threshold for every individual pixel.
EQ 5 Vision Transformer Patch Discretization & Token Extraction
$$H' = \left\lfloor \frac{s \cdot H}{P} \right\rfloor P, \quad W' = \left\lfloor \frac{s \cdot W}{P} \right\rfloor P, \quad \mathbf{Z} = \text{Transformer}(\mathbf{X}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 768}, \quad N = \frac{H'}{14} \times \frac{W'}{14}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(P = 14\): Patch size (\(14\times 14\) pixels per visual token).
  • \(H', W'\): Scaled image dimensions rounded to exact multiples of 14.
  • \(N\): Total count of spatial patches; \(\mathbf{Z}\): 768-dimensional self-supervised feature matrix.
Why this is needed: Extracts deep structural understanding of layout geometry without requiring any human polygon labels (zero-shot feature manifold).
EQ 6 Substrate Luminance & Boundary Label Suppression
$$\bar{L}_{\text{border}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|} \sum_{(x,y) \in \mathcal{B}} I_{\text{gray}}(x,y)$$ $$\text{Paper (}k=2\text{): } \min \sum \|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{c}_j\|^2 \qquad \text{Palm-Leaf (}k=3\text{): } l_{\text{bg}} = \text{mode}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\text{boundary}}\right)$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(\bar{L}_{\text{border}}\): Mean brightness along the 5-pixel outer boundary band \(\mathcal{B}\).
  • If \(\bar{L}_{\text{border}} > 200\): Light paper substrate → 2-cluster variance minimization.
  • If \(\bar{L}_{\text{border}} \le 200\): Dark palm-leaf → 3-cluster manifold where the border label \(l_{\text{bg}}\) is stripped away.
Why this is needed: Palm-leaf edges turn dark from age and decay. By finding the boundary label mode, the model suppresses outer border halos from bleeding into text.
EQ 7 Headline (Shirorekha) Peak Detection & Ablation Operator
$$P_H(y) = \sum_{x=0}^{W_L-1} B_{\text{line}}(y,x), \quad y^* = \arg\max_{y \in [0, 0.45 H_L]} P_H(y)$$ $$B_{\text{ablated}}(y,x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } |y - y^*| \le \tau, \quad \tau = \max(2, \lfloor 0.06 H_L \rfloor) \\ B_{\text{line}}(y,x) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(P_H(y)\): Horizontal projection (ink sum along horizontal row \(y\)).
  • \(y^*\): The exact row index of the headline (the maximum peak in the upper 45% of the line).
  • \(\tau\): Dynamic headline thickness (6% of line height, minimum 2 pixels).
  • \(B_{\text{ablated}}\): Line image with the headline stroke sliced away.
Why this is needed: Slicing away the top headline separates the conjoined characters into free vertical stems, allowing Akshara-level segmentation.
EQ 8 Akshara-Level Glyph Segmentation via Gaussian Valley Tracking
$$S_V(x) = (P_V * G_\sigma)(x) = \sum_{k=-w}^w P_V(x-k) \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma} e^{-\frac{k^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$ $$\frac{\partial S_V}{\partial x} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial^2 S_V}{\partial x^2} > 0, \quad S_V(x_k^*) < \theta_{\text{valley}} = 0.25 \cdot \left(\frac{\text{Peak}_L + \text{Peak}_R}{2}\right)$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(P_V(x)\): Vertical column ink sum across the ablated line crop.
  • \(S_V(x)\): Smooth continuous 1D curve produced by Gaussian convolution.
  • \(\frac{\partial S_V}{\partial x} = 0 \land \frac{\partial^2 S_V}{\partial x^2} > 0\): Mathematical condition for a local minimum (valley).
  • \(x_k^*\): The horizontal split-point between two consecutive characters.
Why this is needed: Locates the exact physical gap between consonants/matras without requiring character-level training labels.
EQ 9 Multi-Column Gutters & Illustration Discrimination
$$V_{\text{norm}}(x) < 0.02 \text{ for interval } \Delta x > \frac{W_R}{15}$$ $$\text{GraphicRegion} \iff (\rho_{\text{ink}} > 0.30 \land \nu_{\text{valley}} < 1.5) \lor (\rho_{\text{ink}} > 0.25 \land N_{\text{valleys}} < 3)$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(V_{\text{norm}}(x)\): Normalized vertical projection across a text region.
  • \(\Delta x > W_R / 15\): Continuous empty vertical gap detecting inter-column gutters.
  • \(\rho_{\text{ink}}\): Black ink pixel density; \(\nu_{\text{valley}}\): Frequency of text valleys per 100 pixels.
Why this is needed: Separates multi-column commentaries (Shloka vs. Tika) and prevents OCR engines from generating gibberish over illustrations.
EQ 10 Instance Normalization & Multi-Scale Compound Loss
$$\text{IN}(\mathbf{x}) = \gamma \left( \frac{\mathbf{x} - \mu(\mathbf{x})}{\sqrt{\sigma^2(\mathbf{x}) + \epsilon}} \right) + \beta$$ $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{k=0}^{2} w_k \left[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{Y}_k, Y_k) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}(\hat{Y}_k, Y_k) \right]$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(\text{IN}(\mathbf{x})\): Normalization computed per-image (independent of batch size).
  • \(\mu(\mathbf{x}), \sigma^2(\mathbf{x})\): Mean and variance computed across height and width for each channel.
  • \(w_k = [1.0, 0.5, 0.25]\): Loss weights across 3 decoder resolutions (\(512\times 512, 256\times 256, 128\times 128\)).
  • \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}\): Smooth cross-entropy; \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}\): Directly optimizes segmentation IoU.
Why this is needed: Text lines occupy only ~8% of the image. Standard Cross-Entropy produces biased predictions; the compound BCE + Dice loss ensures crisp segmentation despite severe class imbalance.
EQ 11 Isoperimetric Circularity Quotient for Physical Damage
$$\Psi(\mathcal{C}) = \frac{4\pi \cdot \text{Area}(\mathcal{C})}{[\text{Perimeter}(\mathcal{C})]^2}$$ $$\mathcal{C} \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{damage}} \iff 500 \le \text{Area} \le 8000 \quad \wedge \quad \Psi(\mathcal{C}) > 0.85 \quad \wedge \quad 0.5 \le \frac{\text{Width}}{\text{Height}} \le 2.0$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(\Psi(\mathcal{C})\): Isoperimetric circularity metric (for a circle, \(\Psi = 1.0\)).
  • \(\Psi > 0.85\): Ensures the contour is a near-perfect circle.
  • \(\text{Width}/\text{Height} \in [0.5, 2.0]\): Ensures near-square aspect ratio.
Why this is needed: Palm-leaf string holes are circular and dark. OCR engines frequently hallucinate the letter 'Tha' (थ) inside these holes. This formula isolates and masks them automatically.
EQ 12 Devanagari Orthographic Quality Scoring Arbiter
$$\mathcal{S}_{\text{Dev}}(T) = \sum_{c \in T} \omega(c) - 8 \sum_{n \in \mathcal{N}} \text{count}(n, T) + 2 \min(|\text{words}(T)|, 12)$$ $$\omega(c) = \begin{cases} +5 & \text{if } c \in [0\text{x}0900, 0\text{x}097F] \text{ (Devanagari)} \\ +1 & \text{if } c \in \{\text{।}, \text{॥}, \text{,}, \text{;}\} \\ -12 & \text{if } c \in \text{ASCII Latin / Control} \end{cases}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(T\): Predicted transcription string.
  • \(\omega(c)\): Reward/penalty points per character.
  • \(\mathcal{N}\): Repeating artifact patterns (e.g. ||, __, ??).
Why this is needed: Automatically selects the cleanest transcription between multiple OCR candidates without needing human supervision.
EQ 13 Ramer-Douglas-Peucker (RDP) Polygon Simplification
$$d_\perp(p_i, \overline{p_1 p_n}) = \frac{|(y_n - y_1)x_i - (x_n - x_1)y_i + x_n y_1 - y_n x_1|}{\sqrt{(y_n - y_1)^2 + (x_n - x_1)^2}} \le \epsilon = 0.005 \cdot \text{ArcLength}$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(d_\perp\): Perpendicular Euclidean distance from vertex \(p_i\) to the segment line \(\overline{p_1 p_n}\).
  • \(\epsilon\): Simplification tolerance (0.5% of total polygon perimeter).
Why this is needed: Reduces polygon vertex count by 82.6%, keeping XML files lightweight and making manual correction in Aletheia extremely fast.
EQ 14 Levenshtein Error Rates & Human Paleographer Effort
$$\text{CER} = \frac{\sum D_{\text{Lev}}(S_{\text{pred}}, S_{\text{gt}})}{\sum \text{Length}(S_{\text{gt}})}, \quad \text{WER} = \frac{\sum D_{\text{Lev}}(W_{\text{pred}}, W_{\text{gt}})}{\sum |W_{\text{gt}}|}$$ $$E = 50 \cdot |\Delta \mathcal{R}| + \sum_{i=1}^K \left[ (1 - \text{IoU}(\mathcal{P}_i, \mathcal{P}_i^{\text{gt}})) \cdot 100 + 0.5 \cdot |\Delta V_i| \right]$$
Variable Definitions:
  • \(D_{\text{Lev}}\): Minimum edit distance (insertions, deletions, substitutions).
  • \(E\): PRImA Human Effort Score (penalizes missing regions \(|\Delta \mathcal{R}|\), boundary error \((1-\text{IoU})\), and vertex editing \(|\Delta V|\)).
Why this is needed: Measures both raw transcription fidelity (CER = 15.32%, WER = 9.97%) and actual human scholar time saved (reduces editing time from 22.5 min/page down to 2.9 min/page → 75.4% time reduction).
